What a forward deployed engineer actually does in a week

My version of the job: sitting between the customer and the code, and staying after launch.

My title is Forward Deployed AI Engineer. People ask what that means, so here is my version, from the inside.

The short answer: I sit between the customer and the code. I learn how a team really works, then I design, build and ship the system that changes it.

Listen

The process document says one thing. The people doing the work do another. The gap between the two is usually where the real problem lives, so I start by watching the work and asking why each step exists.

Some of the most useful questions are small ones. "What do you do when this field is empty?" can uncover a whole workaround nobody wrote down.

Design

Before any code, I sketch what the person will see and click. If a screen is confusing to the people who will use it daily, the clever backend behind it does not matter.

Build

This is the part most people picture: the backend, the integrations with the customer's existing systems, the prompts and the checks around them. The difference from a normal product job is that I build it for one specific group of people I can actually talk to.

You rarely get a spec. You get a problem, and the first version is usually a little wrong in a way you could not have guessed from your desk.

Stay

Launch is not the end. After it goes live, someone has to answer the vendor who cannot log in, explain to a team why a document was flagged, and fix the case nobody tested. Being the person who stays is how you earn trust, and it is also where you learn the most about what you built.

What I would tell someone considering the role

  • Comfort with a vague problem matters more than knowing every tool.
  • You will explain technical choices to people who care about business outcomes. Practice doing that without jargon.
  • It is not the cleanest job. You will sometimes fix something late at night because someone has a deadline tomorrow.
  • You get to see the whole thing, from the first conversation to the day someone stops doing a task by hand. I have not found anything more satisfying.
