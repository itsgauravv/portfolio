Five questions I ask before an AI system runs without a human

A short checklist for deciding whether an AI workflow is ready to run on its own.

A demo works when you are watching it. A production system has to work when you are not. Before I let an AI workflow run without a person in the loop, I ask five questions. If I cannot answer one of them, it is not ready.

1. What happens if the same input arrives twice?

Uploads get retried. Users double-click. Queues deliver twice. If handling the same input twice produces two results, you will eventually do the same thing twice in the real world. Make the work safe to repeat: recognise a duplicate and handle it once.

2. Can I see what the AI changed?

When a field has a value, I want to know whether a person typed it or the AI filled it, and what it was before. A field-level log of changes is what lets someone in operations or finance trust the system, and it is what lets you debug it at all.

3. What does it do when it is unsure?

The right answer is never "guess". Decide ahead of time what low confidence or a failed check does. Usually the answer is to hand it to a person with the reason attached, so the system is allowed to say "I do not know".

4. Can I change its instructions safely?

Prompts are part of the product. You will need to change them in production. I want changes to be reviewable, with a clear before and after, and easy to roll back. If changing a prompt means editing a string and hoping, that is a risk.

5. How do I turn it off?

Eventually something unexpected will happen, such as a new document format or a provider outage. There should be a simple way to pause the automatic path and fall back to manual, without a deploy and without losing the work in progress.

Why these five

None of them are about the model. They are about what surrounds it. In my experience, that is where most of the work is, and it is what turns an impressive demo into something a team can rely on.
