What I changed when I rebuilt my portfolio, resume and LinkedIn

Why I made all three tell the same story, and the small decisions behind each one.

I recently rebuilt three things at once: this website, my resume and my LinkedIn profile. I did them together on purpose, because someone who looks at one will usually look at the others.

One story first

Before changing anything, I wrote down a single sentence about what I do: I build AI systems that run in production, from the interface to the model pipeline. Every change after that had to support that sentence or it got cut.

The portfolio

The site is one page with one typeface and almost no color. I wanted it to load fast and read in a minute. Each project has a short summary at the top for someone skimming, and the detail below for someone who wants it.

There is also a constraint I could not avoid: I cannot name the company I built these systems for. So I describe the problem and what I built, not the logo. That turned out to be a useful discipline, because it forces the writing to be about the work.

The resume

One page. Each line says what I built and what changed because of it. I removed anything I could not explain in an interview. If a bullet needs a paragraph of context to make sense, it does not belong on a one-page resume.

LinkedIn

The headline now says what I do and the main tools I use, instead of just a job title. Recruiters search by keywords, and a person skimming a search result gets the point in one line. I also removed the open-to-work banner and made sure the link to the site is the first thing in my profile.

What I would do again

  • Write the one-sentence story before touching any design.
  • Make the first screen of everything readable in under a minute.
  • Keep every claim something I can talk about for five minutes.

What is still unfinished

Product screenshots for the case studies, recommendations on LinkedIn, and more writing like this. I would rather ship the page and keep improving it than wait until it is perfect.
